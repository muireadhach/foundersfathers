This was much spoken of as a useful piece and gave rise to a project which soon followed it, of forming a company for the more ready extinguishing of fires and mutual assistance in removing and securing of goods when in danger. Associates in this scheme were presently found, amounting to thirty. Our articles of agreement oblig’d every member to keep always in good order and fit for use, a certain number of leather buckets with strong bags and baskets (for packing and transporting of goods), which were to be brought to every fire; and we agreed to meet once a month and spend a social evening together, in discoursing and communicating such ideas as occurred to us upon the subject of fires as might be useful in our conduct on such occasions.

Benjamin Franklin, The Works of Benjamin Franklin, Vol. I Autobiography, Letters and Misc. Writings 1725-1734 THE AUTOBIOGRAPHY › 13.: Humility

Scripture anchor Galatians 6:9-10

Why it is here

The cell's phase1 founders seed — the fire company — in Franklin's own account: thirty associates, each keeping buckets and bags 'in good order and fit for use,' meeting monthly. A household-scale venture serving the community, with 'household-scale to civic-scale' (cell vocabulary) visible in the sequel: companies multiplied 'till they became so numerous as to include most of the inhabitants.' Anchored to Galatians 6:9-10, do good to all.

Benjamin Franklin, The Works of Benjamin Franklin, Vol. I Autobiography, Letters and Misc. Writings 1725-1734 CORRESPONDENCE AND MISCELLANEOUS WRITINGS › VII: RULES FOR A CLUB ESTABLISHED FOR MUTUAL IMPROVEMENT

Scripture anchor Hebrews 13:16

Why it is here

The Junto's standing queries (seed): a small circle asking every meeting how it 'may be serviceable to mankind,' which stranger to oblige, which young beginner to encourage. The betterment project institutionalized as a weekly question — the founders' counterpart to Clement's 'inquire oneself who deserves to receive kindness.' Anchored to Hebrews 13:16 (do not neglect doing good and sharing).
